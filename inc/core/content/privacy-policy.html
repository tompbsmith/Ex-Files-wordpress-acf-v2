6. Conflict checks and personalised guide logic

Our website compares the identifying details supplied at registration, including relevant former-partner details, against a restricted conflict register. A clear match can prevent an online booking; an uncertain match is referred for review. The purpose is to avoid conflicting engagements and protect confidential information. A match is not proof that a named person is or has been our client. We use a general response and do not disclose another person’s relationship with us.

If the check prevents you from proceeding, you can email info@theexfiles.co.uk to ask Rachael to review the decision, explain your circumstances or correct an apparent error. A person with authority to change the outcome will consider the request. We may be unable to disclose confidential reasons, but will explain what we lawfully can.

The free guide uses rules based on your questionnaire answers, including your connections with England and Wales, to select relevant sections and display eligibility information. This affects the guidance shown and may indicate that we cannot confirm eligibility. It is based on the answers provided and is not a court determination. You can ask us to review an apparent error or explain an outcome.

We do not use special category information to make solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. Where an automated outcome otherwise has such effects, we apply the safeguards required by the UK GDPR, including information about the decision and opportunities to make representations, obtain human intervention and contest it.

7. Consultations and recordings

Consultations take place using Microsoft Teams. Meeting invitations and reminders contain your name, the service, date, time and meeting link. We aim to use minimal, neutral calendar information and avoid including detailed family or financial information in invitations.

We do not record a consultation merely because you have accepted our terms or attended the meeting. Before an optional recording, we explain its purpose, who can access it, whether transcription is involved and how long it will be kept, and obtain the necessary separate agreement from participants. You can decline recording without losing access to the consultation, or ask us to stop. We can instead take written notes.

We do not enable AI meeting summaries or other AI processing of your matter without first assessing the provider and lawful basis, updating the relevant information and obtaining consent where required. An ordinary recording consent does not automatically authorise a separate AI use.

10. How long we keep information

We retain information only for as long as justified by its purpose. The following schedule applies unless a specific legal obligation, complaint, dispute, safeguarding concern or legal hold justifies a different period. We document exceptions and review their continuing necessity.

Free Divorce Guide requests and questionnaire answers

Delete or anonymise no later than 24 months after delivery; earlier where the purpose has ended and there is no justification to retain them

Client matter files, substantive preliminary advice and associated correspondence

Normally retain for 6 years from closure or the end of the substantive engagement, then review promptly for secure deletion or anonymisation; retain longer only for a documented, matter-specific reason

Minimal conflict register for substantive prospective and actual engagements

Review entries every 6 years from matter closure or, where no matter is opened, from the last substantive interaction. Retain a limited record only while an identified continuing confidentiality or conflict risk makes it necessary. Review sooner where a rights request, correction or material change in circumstances calls its continued retention into question; securely delete or anonymise information that is no longer needed.

Routine appointment recordings and transcripts, if separately agreed

Delete within 90 days of the consultation after preparing and checking any necessary written record, unless an identified complaint, dispute or other documented legal reason justifies longer retention

Tax and accounting records

Keep for the period required by the tax rules applicable to our sole-trader business, normally at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline for the relevant tax year; longer where a specific rule or enquiry requires it

Website Activity Log and related technical and security records

Activity Log records, including the IP address of the customer who performed the recorded action, are normally retained for 6 years from the date of that action to provide an audit trail of website and customer activity, investigate misuse or disputes and protect legal rights. Other technical and security records are kept only for as long as necessary for their documented purpose. We assess requests for earlier deletion individually and delete or anonymise records at the end of the applicable period unless a documented legal reason justifies longer retention.

Privacy requests and complaint handling records

Normally 6 years after resolution, subject to necessity and proportionality review and removal of unnecessary underlying material

Payment records may remain in our business accounts after a client portal account is closed where required for accounting or other justified purposes. If information from a guide or enquiry becomes relevant to a client engagement, the relevant material follows the client-file schedule, rather than remaining as an unnecessary duplicate.

Preliminary registration details are saved before payment to enable conflict checking and order review; a full login account is completed after purchase, including email confirmation. Records relating to an accepted client engagement follow the client-matter retention period. If a proposed order does not proceed, preliminary registration information is kept only for as long as necessary to complete the conflict or order review, resolve any related query or meet another documented purpose, and is then securely deleted or anonymised. Separately justified minimal conflict records and Activity Log entries follow their respective retention rules above. A name-only preliminary conflict check does not automatically create a permanent conflict or justify keeping former-partner details indefinitely. Substantive confidential information received before a paid engagement can, however, justify a limited continuing conflict record.

A six-year review is not a promise to retain every document forever. Longer retention may be justified by the nature of a claim, a child’s involvement, capacity issues, a continuing obligation or a live dispute; it is assessed individually. A deletion request is considered against the actual information and purpose, rather than refused automatically.

Deletion covers live systems and duplicates under our control. Backup copies are isolated from ordinary use and expire through the backup cycle. If restored, applicable deletions are reapplied. Copies held by independent controllers are subject to their own lawful retention obligations. Your Ex File is not a permanent archive; please retain your own copies of important documents.

12. Cookies, website technologies and marketing

Our website only uses cookies that are strictly necessary for it to work, so they do not need your consent. WordPress sets login cookies that keep you signed in to your Ex File until you close your browser (or for 14 days if you tick “remember me”), and a test cookie that checks your browser accepts cookies. Our own basket cookie remembers the service and details you chose while you pay, for 48 hours. When you pay, Stripe sets its own cookies on its payment page for security and fraud prevention, as explained in Stripe’s privacy policy. You can block or delete cookies in your browser settings, but the account and payment pages may not work without them.

Where consent is required, optional technologies are not activated until you choose them. You can reject optional technologies or change your choice through the website controls as easily as accepting them. Some technologies may qualify for a statutory exception; where we rely on an exception, we meet its conditions, including information and any required means of objection. Blocking essential technologies may prevent account, booking or payment functions from working.

We do not currently send marketing emails. Requesting a guide, creating an account or purchasing a service does not enrol you in a marketing list. If you start a booking but do not complete payment, we may send you one reminder email about that booking, unless you tick the box at checkout asking us not to; you can also reply to that email to tell us not to contact you again. Service emails include information needed to provide the guide, administer your account or deliver a purchased service. If we introduce marketing, we will explain it separately, establish the required lawful basis and PECR permission, and provide a straightforward way to unsubscribe. Marketing consent will be optional and separate from service terms.

13. Your rights

Depending on the circumstances, you may:

  • Ask whether we process your information and obtain a copy and supporting information.
  • Ask us to correct inaccurate information or complete incomplete information.
  • Ask us to erase information or restrict its use.
  • Object to processing based on legitimate interests, including related profiling. We must stop unless we demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds overriding your interests, rights and freedoms, or the processing is needed for legal claims.
  • Receive information you supplied in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format, and request transfer where the portability right applies to automated processing based on consent or contract.
  • Withdraw consent at any time where we rely on it. This does not make earlier lawful processing unlawful. We explain any effect on an optional activity; withdrawing recording consent does not end the consultation service.
  • Obtain the applicable safeguards for automated decisions described above.
  • Object absolutely to any use of your information for direct marketing if we introduce it.

Email info@theexfiles.co.uk to exercise a right. You do not need legal wording or a particular form. We normally respond without undue delay and within one calendar month. Where the law allows an extension of up to two further months for complexity or the number of requests, we explain this and the reason within the initial period. We may ask for proportionate identity or authority evidence, or clarification where legally justified, and explain any lawful effect on the response period. Requests are normally free; any permitted fee or refusal will be explained.

Rights are not absolute. We may need to retain information for a legal obligation or legal claim, and must consider other people’s rights, confidentiality and any applicable legal privilege or statutory exemption. We assess restrictions case by case and explain our decision as far as we lawfully can. Confidentiality is not a blanket exemption from data protection rights.

If you provide information about someone else, that person has rights in their own information. This does not automatically entitle them to your entire file or confidential advice.
